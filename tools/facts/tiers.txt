[Napping Kitten]
Newborn kittens sleep up to 20 hours a day, and the growth hormone that builds them is released while they sleep.
Kittens are born deaf and blind. Their eyes and ears open during the second week.
An adult cat sleeps 12 to 16 hours a day, roughly two thirds of its life.
Cats dream. Their sleep has the same REM stage as ours, twitching whiskers included.
Newborn kittens cannot keep themselves warm, so they sleep in a heap against their mother and each other.
A kitten is born weighing around 100 grams, about the same as a bar of soap.
Newborn kittens feed every two to three hours around the clock and sleep most of the time in between.
Newborn kittens twitch and squirm in their sleep. The twitching is thought to help wire up their nerves and muscles.
Newborn kittens find a teat by warmth and smell, and many go back to the same one at every feed.
Newborn kittens cannot go to the toilet on their own. Their mother licks their bellies to get them started.
A mother cat's first milk, called colostrum, passes on antibodies that protect her kittens for their first weeks.
Mother cats move kittens to a new nest one by one, carrying each by the scruff. The kitten goes limp and still.
Kittens are born with a rooting reflex, pushing their noses into anything warm and soft in search of milk.
A mother cat purrs while nursing, and her kittens feel the vibration before they can see or hear her.
A kitten's eyes do not open all at once. They part gradually over a few days, sometimes one eye ahead of the other.
A litter is usually three to five kittens, though a single kitten or a litter of eight is not unheard of.
[Sleepy Kitten]
Kittens start purring at a few days old, mostly to tell their mother they are fed and warm.
A dozing cat keeps its ears on duty. They swivel toward sounds while the rest of it stays asleep.
Kittens roughly double their birth weight in their first one to two weeks.
The slow blink a cat gives you is relaxed body language. Blink slowly back and many cats answer in kind.
A cat's purr comes from the voice box muscles twitching 25 to 150 times a second, on both the in breath and the out.
Kittens knead with their front paws while nursing to get the milk flowing. Many adult cats still knead a soft lap.
A cat curled into a tight ball is keeping warm. The pose covers the belly and paws and holds in body heat.
A cat runs warmer than a person, with a normal body temperature of about 38 to 39 C.
A 2020 study found cats more likely to approach a stranger who slow blinked at them than one who did not.
A kitten asleep on its back with its belly showing feels safe. The belly is a cat's most vulnerable spot.
A content cat may drool a little while kneading and purring, a leftover from its nursing days.
Cats barely sweat. They lose heat through their paw pads and by panting, and stretch out flat when too warm.
Cats have a special purr for asking. It hides a cry near the pitch of a baby's, and people find it hard to ignore.
Young kittens switch off fast. A kitten can go from racing around the room to fast asleep within a minute.
Cats follow the sun around a room through the day. A sunny patch of floor keeps a cat warm without burning energy.
[Playful Kitten]
Kitten play is hunting practice. The stalk, the pounce and the kill bite are all rehearsed on toys and siblings.
All kittens are born with blue eyes. The adult colour settles in at six to seven weeks.
A cat's whiskers are about as wide as its body, which is how it knows whether it fits through a gap.
Kittens learn to retract their claws at around four weeks. Before that they stick to everything.
Kittens take their first wobbly steps at about three weeks old. By six weeks they run, climb and pounce on each other.
A kitten has 26 milk teeth. The 30 adult teeth replace them between about three and six months of age.
Weaning begins at about four weeks, when kittens first nibble solid food, and is mostly done by eight weeks.
Kitten wrestling and chasing begins at about four weeks and peaks at around three months, then gives way to solo play.
Mother cats bring prey back to the nest for kittens from about four to five weeks old, dead at first and then alive.
Kittens learn hunting partly by watching. Kittens raised by a hunting mother pick up the skill faster than those without.
Kittens' ears are folded flat at birth. They unfold and stand up over the first few weeks and start to swivel toward sounds.
A newly opened kitten eye is cloudy blue and sees poorly. Clear sight takes several more weeks to develop.
Kittens are born with an urge to dig and bury. Most use a litter tray from about four weeks old with no training at all.
The righting reflex that lets a cat land on its feet appears at three to four weeks and is complete by about seven.
Cats favour a paw much as people favour a hand. In one study most females used the right paw and most males the left.
Kittens are most open to new people and places between about two and seven weeks. Gentle handling then makes a friendlier cat.
Kittens put on roughly 100 grams a week for their first few months and reach adult size at about a year old.
[House Cat]
A house cat can hit about 48 km/h in a sprint, faster than Usain Bolt at his quickest.
Each cat ear has about 32 muscles and turns 180 degrees, so a cat can aim one ear at you and one at the fridge.
Cats cannot taste sweetness. They lack a working sweet receptor gene, so dessert means nothing to them.
Cats moved in with people about 10,000 years ago, drawn to the mice in the first grain stores of the Near East.
Cats see well in about a sixth of the light people need, helped by a mirror layer behind the retina that bounces light back.
Cats have a second scent organ in the roof of the mouth. The open-mouthed grimace after a sniff is the cat using it.
A cat's tongue is covered in tiny hooked spines of keratin, the stuff of fingernails, that comb fur and scrape meat off bone.
A cat has around 230 bones, some two dozen more than a person. Most of the extras are in its tail and bendy spine.
A cat walks on its toes. The joint that looks like a backward knee on the hind leg is really its heel.
A cat's meow is mostly for people. Adult cats rarely meow at each other, but learn fast that it works on humans.
The average pet cat lives 12 to 15 years, and cats kept indoors usually live longer than those that roam.
Cats have only around 470 taste buds, against about 9,000 in a person. Smell does most of the work at mealtimes.
An upright tail with a little curl at the tip is a cat's friendly greeting. A fast lashing tail means the mood has turned.
Cats scratch to leave scent and visible marks and to shed the worn outer layer of their claws, not to sharpen them.
Cats drink by flicking the tongue tip against the water and biting off the column it pulls up, about four laps a second.
A cat's pupils shrink to slits in bright light and almost fill the eye in the dark, a far bigger change than a person's.
[Alley Cat]
A cat can squeeze through any gap its head fits. Its collarbone floats free, attached only by muscle.
Cats right themselves in mid-air in a fraction of a second, then spread out like a parachute to slow a long fall.
A cat rubbing its cheek on a wall or your leg is leaving scent from glands around its mouth. That is mine now.
Feral cats in a colony share a territory and even nurse each other's kittens, but every one of them hunts alone.
Rome treats its street cats as part of the city's heritage, and a famous colony lives in the ruins where Julius Caesar was killed.
Istanbul's street cats are fed and sheltered by the whole city. The 2016 documentary Kedi followed seven of them.
Tombili, a plump Istanbul street cat famous for lounging on a step, got a bronze statue in his favourite spot in 2016.
Trap-neuter-return means catching street cats, neutering them and putting them back. In time the colony stops growing.
A neutered feral cat usually has the tip of one ear clipped, so helpers can tell from a distance that it has been done.
A stray is a pet that lost its home and can often be tamed again. A true feral grew up wild and treats people as a threat.
Feral colonies form around a food source. Related females often stay together for life, while young males wander off.
A female cat can have two or three litters a year, which is why a few unneutered strays become a colony so fast.
Ship's cats kept rats off the grain for centuries and reached ports all over the world, where plenty jumped ship and went stray.
Feral cats are not a wild species. They are the same animal as the pet on the sofa, Felis catus, just unused to people.
Japan's cat islands, such as Aoshima, have far more cats than people. Fishermen brought them to deal with the mice.
Gli, the resident cat of Istanbul's Hagia Sophia, was photographed by tourists for years and died in 2020 aged 16.
[Wildcat]
Every house cat descends from the African wildcat, Felis lybica, which still roams the savanna looking like a lean tabby.
The Scottish wildcat, the Highland tiger, is Britain's last native cat and one of the rarest animals on the island.
A wildcat looks like a big tabby, but its thick blunt tail has dark rings and a solid black tip.
Wildcats hunt at dawn and dusk and will take a rabbit nearly as heavy as themselves.
A European wildcat is a bit bigger than a house cat, with a broader head, a thicker coat and faint stripes but no spots.
Wildcats shun people completely. Even wildcat kittens raised by hand stay wary and almost never tame.
Scottish wildcats have mixed so much with pet cats that a 2019 review called the wild population functionally extinct.
Captive-bred wildcats were released into the Cairngorms in 2023, the first step in putting the species back in the Highlands.
The African wildcat is sandy or grey with faint stripes and reddish backs to its ears, and ranges across Africa into Asia.
Wildcats live alone. Each adult holds its own territory, marked with scent, and meets others mainly to breed.
A European wildcat litter is usually three or four kittens, born in spring in a hollow tree, rock crevice or old burrow.
Wildcats once lived across Britain, but hunting and forest clearance had confined them to the Scottish Highlands by the late 1800s.
European wildcats range from Scotland and Spain east to Turkey and the Caucasus, mostly in forests with plenty of cover.
In 2017 cat experts split the wildcat into two species: Felis silvestris in Europe and Felis lybica in Africa and Asia.
Wildcats and domestic cats can breed together, and their kittens are fertile, which is what blurs the line between the two.
A wildcat with its claws out is the crest of Scotland's Clan Chattan, whose motto warns: touch not the cat without a glove.
[Bobcat]
The bobcat is named for its stubby, bobbed tail, around 15 cm long.
A bobcat can leap about 3 metres onto prey and has been known to bring down deer several times its own weight.
Bobcats are North America's most common wild cat, with a few million roaming the United States.
A bobcat stalks to within a few metres, then takes its prey in one short burst. Patience first, speed second.
Bobcats live right across the United States, into southern Canada and down into Mexico, in deserts, swamps and suburbs alike.
A bobcat is roughly twice the size of a house cat, usually 7 to 14 kg, with short ear tufts and a ruff of fur on each cheek.
The bobcat is a lynx. Its scientific name is Lynx rufus, the red lynx, after the reddish tint in its coat.
A bobcat's tail tip is black on top and white underneath. A Canada lynx's tail tip is black all the way round.
A male bobcat's territory can cover dozens of square kilometres, marked with urine, droppings and scratched trees.
Bobcat kittens are born in spring, usually two or three to a litter, and stay with their mother for most of their first year.
Rabbits and hares are the bobcat's main food, but it will eat anything from mice and birds to deer and the odd chicken.
Bobcats are shy, mostly active at dawn and dusk, and most people never see one even where they are common.
The bobcat is rated Least Concern by the IUCN, with numbers stable or growing across most of its range.
Bobcats have adapted to suburbs and city edges, and are studied in the hills around Los Angeles.
A bobcat's spotted coat shifts with the seasons, greyer in winter and more reddish in summer.
Bobcats hide what they cannot finish of a kill under leaves or snow and come back to it for days.
[Lynx]
A lynx's huge furry paws work like snowshoes, spreading its weight so it can run across deep snow.
Canada lynx live mostly on snowshoe hares, and their numbers rise and fall with the hares on a roughly ten-year cycle.
The black tufts on a lynx's ears grow up to 4 cm long. They probably sharpen its hearing and signal to other lynxes.
The Eurasian lynx is the biggest of the four lynxes, up to 30 kg, and can take down a roe deer.
There are four lynx species: the Eurasian lynx, the Canada lynx, the Iberian lynx and the bobcat.
The Iberian lynx fell to fewer than 100 animals in 2002. Captive breeding and releases pushed it past 2,000 by 2024.
The Iberian lynx eats almost nothing but rabbits, so when disease wiped out Spain's rabbits the lynx nearly went with them.
The Eurasian lynx lives from Scandinavia and the Alps right across Russia to the Pacific coast and the Himalayas.
Lynx have very short tails, around 10 to 20 cm, with a solid black tip.
The Eurasian lynx was hunted out of most of Western Europe by the early 1900s and has since been put back in the Alps and Harz.
A lynx's ruff of long fur around the face looks like a beard and grows fuller in winter.
The name lynx comes from an old Greek word linked to light, a nod to the way the cat's eyes shine in the dark.
Canada lynx were reintroduced to Colorado from 1999, and the species was listed as threatened in the lower 48 states in 2000.
The Iberian lynx lives only in Spain and Portugal. It is spotted, bearded and about half the weight of a Eurasian lynx.
Lynx kittens are born in late spring, usually two or three to a litter, and stay with their mother through their first winter.
A lynx hunts by stalking or ambush. It cannot run far, so a chase that misses in the first few bounds is given up.
[Ocelot]
No two ocelots have the same coat. The chains of spots and streaks are as unique as a fingerprint.
Ocelots are strong swimmers and often hunt along riverbanks at night, fish and crabs included.
Salvador Dali kept a pet ocelot called Babou and brought it along to restaurants.
An ocelot hunts in near darkness by sight. Its eyes gather far more light than ours.
Ocelots range from southern Texas down through Mexico and Central America to northern Argentina.
Fewer than 100 ocelots are left in the United States, nearly all of them in the thorn scrub of southern Texas.
An ocelot weighs 8 to 15 kg, about twice a house cat, and stands roughly 40 to 50 cm at the shoulder.
In the 1960s ocelot fur coats were in fashion and well over 100,000 skins were sold each year. The trade is banned today.
Ocelots rest in trees or thick bush by day and hunt at night, taking rodents, rabbits, birds, lizards and frogs.
Ocelots live alone, and a male's territory often overlaps the ranges of two or three females.
Ocelot kittens usually arrive one at a time, sometimes two, and take about two years to become fully independent.
The ocelot's name comes from the Aztec word ocelotl, which the Aztecs themselves used for the jaguar.
The IUCN lists the ocelot as Least Concern across its whole range, but it is an endangered species in the United States.
Ocelots mark their territory with scent and by leaving droppings in shared latrine spots along their trails.
Ocelots climb well and will hunt in the trees, though most of their prey is caught on the forest floor.
Roads are the biggest killer of Texas ocelots, so wildlife underpasses have been built beneath the highways they cross.
[Serval]
A serval has the longest legs of any cat relative to its body, and the biggest ears.
Servals leap up to 3 metres straight up to snatch birds out of the air.
A serval can hear a rodent moving underground, then dig it out with its long front legs.
Servals catch about half of what they pounce on, the best hit rate of any wild cat.
Servals live across the grasslands and wetlands of Africa south of the Sahara, with only a tiny foothold left in North Africa.
A serval stands about 60 cm at the shoulder, very tall for a cat its weight, on legs made for peering over long grass.
A serval weighs 9 to 18 kg and has a small head, a short tail and golden fur with black spots and shoulder stripes.
Servals eat mostly rodents, and a single serval can catch thousands of them in a year.
The Savannah cat is a cross between a serval and a domestic cat, first bred in the 1980s.
Servals pounce high and land on prey with both front feet to stun it before the bite.
Servals wade into shallow water and hook out frogs and fish with their long front paws.
Serval kittens are born in a burrow or dense grass, usually two or three, and hunt for themselves by about a year old.
The white bars on the backs of a serval's black ears are thought to help kittens follow their mother through long grass.
The name serval comes through French from the Portuguese lobo-cerval, an old word for the lynx.
Black servals are rare but real, and are seen most often in the highlands of East Africa.
A serval's height comes from stretched foot bones below the ankle, not from a longer thigh.
[Caracal]
The caracal's name comes from the Turkish karakulak, which means black ear.
Caracals were trained in Persia and India to hunt birds, leaping into a flock and knocking down several at once.
A caracal can jump more than 3 metres into the air and bat a bird down with a paw.
Caracals can go a long time without drinking, taking their water from their prey.
Caracals live across Africa, the Middle East and into India, in dry scrub, savanna and semi-desert.
A caracal's black ear tufts are around 4 to 5 cm long, and its ears flick and swivel constantly as it listens.
Caracals weigh 8 to 18 kg and have a plain reddish tan coat with no spots, long legs and a short tail.
The caracal is nicknamed the desert lynx but is not a lynx. Its closest relatives are the serval and the African golden cat.
Caracals can take prey heavier than themselves, including small antelope, and sometimes stash a kill in a tree.
A caracal litter is usually two or three kittens, born in a burrow or rock den, with eyes that open within about ten days.
Caracals appear in ancient Egyptian art, painted on tomb walls and carved in stone.
Caracals hunt mainly at night and in the cool of dawn and dusk, and rest under bushes or in rock crevices by day.
A caracal's hind legs are longer than its front legs, which is where its straight-up spring comes from.
In South Africa the caracal is called the rooikat, Afrikaans for red cat.
Caracals roam the slopes of Cape Town's Table Mountain, right up to the suburbs, where a research project tracks them.
Caracals eat rodents, hares, hyraxes and birds, and in farmland sometimes lambs, which gets them shot as pests.
[Puma]
The puma holds the Guinness record for the most names of any animal: cougar, mountain lion, panther, catamount and 40 more.
A puma can jump about 5 metres straight up and 12 metres along the ground in a single bound.
Pumas cannot roar. They purr, hiss, growl, chirp and scream.
Pumas have the largest range of any land mammal in the Americas, from the Yukon down to the tip of Patagonia.
The puma is the largest of the small cats. It sits on the same branch of the cat family as the house cat, not the lions.
The Florida panther is a puma. Fewer than 250 remain in the wild, all in the south of the state.
A puma takes a deer roughly every week or two, hides the carcass under leaves and sticks, and comes back to feed for days.
Puma cubs are born spotted and blue-eyed. The spots fade during the first year and the eyes turn amber or gold.
A puma's hind legs are far longer than its front legs, the biggest difference of any cat, which powers its leaps.
Puma attacks on people are very rare: a handful a year across the whole of North America, and deaths rarer still.
Pumas once ranged across North America, but hunting wiped them out east of the Mississippi except for Florida's small group.
In 2011 a young male puma was killed on a Connecticut road after walking more than 2,000 km from South Dakota.
The puma P-22 lived for ten years in Los Angeles's Griffith Park, hemmed in by freeways, and died in 2022.
Puma cubs stay with their mother for 18 months to two years, learning to hunt before setting out on their own.
The puma is the second heaviest cat in the Americas, after the jaguar.
The puma's scientific name is Puma concolor, meaning one colour, for its plain tawny coat.
[Leopard]
A leopard can haul a kill heavier than itself up a tree, out of reach of lions and hyenas.
Leopards are the most widespread big cat, found from the African savanna to the Russian Far East.
A black panther in Africa or Asia is a leopard with dark fur. Look closely and the rosettes are still there.
Leopard rosettes are such good camouflage that one can lie in short grass a few metres away, unseen.
The Amur leopard of the Russian Far East is the rarest big cat, with around 100 left in the wild.
Leopards are the best climbers of the big cats and often spend the day draped over a high branch.
Leopard size varies hugely. A male in Africa's Cape mountains may weigh 30 kg, while a savanna male can top 80 kg.
Leopards hunt mostly at night and eat almost anything, from dung beetles and fish to antelope heavier than themselves.
Leopard cubs are usually born two to a litter, and stay with their mother for up to two years before heading off alone.
Leopards once lived across much of Europe, and still survive in Turkey, the Caucasus and Iran.
A leopard's call is a rasping cough, often compared to sawing wood, used to tell the neighbours it is around.
Mumbai's Sanjay Gandhi National Park is home to dozens of leopards living alongside a city of over 20 million people.
The IUCN lists the leopard as Vulnerable, and several of its subspecies are Critically Endangered.
The leopard is one of Africa's Big Five, with the lion, elephant, rhino and buffalo, the animals once most prized by hunters.
The snow leopard is not a true leopard. Its closest living relative is the tiger.
The word leopard comes from Greek, lion plus pard, because ancient writers thought it was a cross between the two.
[Jaguar]
For its size the jaguar has the strongest bite of any big cat. It kills by punching through the skull.
Jaguars love water. They swim rivers and hunt caiman and turtles in them.
Jaguar rosettes have spots inside them. Leopard rosettes do not.
The jaguar is the only big cat in the Americas and the third largest cat in the world, after the tiger and lion.
Jaguars range from Mexico to northern Argentina, and about half of all the world's jaguars live in Brazil.
The jaguars of Brazil's Pantanal wetland are the largest anywhere, with big males weighing more than 100 kg.
Jaguars once roamed the southern United States. Today a lone male now and then wanders into Arizona from Mexico.
The jaguar's name comes from yaguara, a word from the Tupi-Guarani languages of South America.
Black jaguars are fairly common, especially in dense forest, and their rosettes still show faintly in good light.
Jaguars hunt by day as well as by night, ambushing prey on the ground, from trees and in the water.
A jaguar is stockier than a leopard, with a bigger head, shorter legs and a shorter tail.
Jaguar cubs are born blind, usually two to a litter, and stay with their mother for around two years.
The Maya and Aztecs revered the jaguar, and elite Aztec jaguar warriors wore its skin into battle.
The IUCN lists the jaguar as Near Threatened, and it has lost about half of its original range.
Jaguars eat more than 85 kinds of prey, from armadillos and capybaras to deer and tapirs.
In 2016 a jaguar called El Jefe was filmed in Arizona's mountains, the only wild jaguar known in the United States at the time.
[Tiger]
The tiger is the biggest cat alive. A large male Amur tiger can weigh more than 250 kg.
A tiger's stripes are on its skin as well as its fur, and no two tigers share a pattern.
Tigers are strong swimmers and will cross rivers several kilometres wide.
A tiger's roar carries about 3 km, and part of it is pitched below the range of human hearing.
Roughly 4,500 to 5,500 tigers live in the wild today, up from a low of about 3,200 around 2010.
India is home to about three quarters of the world's wild tigers, more than 3,000 of them.
A tiger's canine teeth can be over 7 cm long, the longest of any living cat.
Tigers hunt alone, mostly at night, and only about one hunt in ten ends in a kill.
Three tiger subspecies went extinct in the 20th century: the Bali tiger, the Caspian tiger and the Javan tiger.
A tiger can eat up to 40 kg of meat in one sitting, then go for days without another meal.
White tigers are Bengal tigers with a rare recessive gene, and nearly all of them descend from one cub caught in India in 1951.
Tiger cubs are born blind, usually two to four to a litter, and stay with their mother for about two years.
The Sumatran tiger is the smallest tiger alive and the only one left on an Indonesian island.
Tigers and lions can breed in captivity, producing ligers and tigons, but their ranges no longer overlap in the wild.
Tigers greet each other with a chuff, a soft snort made by blowing air through the nose.
Tigers once ranged from Turkey to Korea, and have lost around 93 percent of that range.
[Cheetah]
A cheetah goes from standing to 100 km/h in about three seconds, quicker than most sports cars.
At full sprint a cheetah covers up to 7 metres a stride and is airborne twice in every one.
Cheetahs cannot roar. They chirp like birds and purr like house cats.
A cheetah's tail works as a rudder, swinging out to steer it through sharp turns at full speed.
The black tear marks under a cheetah's eyes are thought to cut the glare of the sun, like a footballer's eye black.
Sarah, a cheetah at Cincinnati Zoo, ran 100 metres in 5.95 seconds in 2012, the fastest time ever recorded for a land animal.
A cheetah's sprint lasts only around 20 to 30 seconds, after which it needs a long rest to recover.
A cheetah's claws only partly retract, like a dog's, and grip the ground like the spikes on running shoes.
Cheetah cubs have a mantle of long grey fur down their backs, thought to make them look like a honey badger to predators.
Only about 7,000 cheetahs are left in the wild, most of them in southern and eastern Africa.
The Asiatic cheetah survives only in Iran, where fewer than 50 remain.
Cheetahs hunt by day, mostly in the morning and late afternoon, which keeps them clear of lions and hyenas.
Lions and hyenas steal many cheetah kills, so a cheetah eats fast and gives up its food rather than fight.
Male cheetahs often live in small groups called coalitions, usually brothers, while females live alone.
The cheetah died out in India in the 1950s. In 2022 cheetahs from Namibia were flown in to start a new population.
The king cheetah has stripes and blotches instead of spots, caused by the same gene that makes a blotched tabby house cat.
All cheetahs alive today descend from a small group that survived a population crash around 10,000 years ago.
[Nyan Cat]
Nyan Cat is a Pop-Tart cat flying through space on a rainbow, drawn in April 2011 and watched hundreds of millions of times.
Nyan means meow in Japanese.
Nyan Cat was drawn during a charity livestream when viewers asked for a cat and a Pop-Tart. The artist drew both at once.
The model for Nyan Cat was the artist's own cat Marty, a Russian Blue.
The original Nyan Cat GIF sold as an NFT in February 2021 for 300 ether, close to 600,000 dollars at the time.
Christopher Torres drew the animation on 2 April 2011 and posted it on his comic site LOL-Comics as Pop Tart Cat.
Nyan Cat's creator Christopher Torres, from Dallas, Texas, posted his comics online under the handle PRguitarman.
The tune is Nyanyanyanyanyanyanya, written by the Japanese music producer daniwell and released in 2010.
The voice singing nyan over and over in the video is Momone Momo, a synthetic singing voice, not a real person.

In 2013 Torres and the owner of Keyboard Cat sued over the two memes' use in the game Scribblenauts. The case was settled.
The official Nyan Cat website plays the animation on a loop and keeps a timer of how long the visitor has been watching.
Fans made Tac Nayn, an evil mirror image of Nyan Cat with its name spelled backwards.
The original YouTube video runs for three minutes and 37 seconds of looping cat and song.
