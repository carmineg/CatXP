Lions are the only truly social cats, living in family groups called prides.
A lion pride is built around related females. The lionesses usually stay for life while males come and go.
A lion's roar can be heard up to 8 km (5 miles) away.
A male lion's mane grows darker and fuller as he matures, and lionesses tend to prefer males with darker manes.
Lionesses do most of the hunting for a pride, often working together to surround prey.
Wild lions survive today in sub-Saharan Africa and in one small population in India's Gir Forest.
Asiatic lions have a fold of skin along the belly that African lions usually lack, and males have thinner manes.
Lions spend most of the day resting and may sleep or doze for up to 20 hours.
White lions are not albinos. Their pale coat comes from a recessive gene found in South Africa's Timbavati area.
Lion cubs are born with faint spots on their coat, and the spots usually fade as they grow up.
The lion is the only cat with a tuft of dark hair at the tip of its tail.
Lions are the only cats in which males and females look clearly different, thanks to the male's mane.
A snow leopard's tail is nearly as long as its body and helps it balance on steep, rocky slopes.
Snow leopards wrap their thick tails around their bodies like a scarf to keep warm when resting.
Snow leopards live in the mountains of Central and South Asia, often above 3,000 metres (about 10,000 feet).
Unlike lions and tigers, a snow leopard cannot roar. It makes a puffing sound called a chuff instead.
Snow leopards are so hard to spot in the wild that they are nicknamed the Ghost of the Mountains.
A snow leopard's wide, furry paws act like snowshoes, spreading its weight on deep snow.
Snow leopards have large nasal cavities that warm the thin, cold mountain air before it reaches the lungs.
Snow leopards are powerful jumpers, reported to clear gaps of up to 15 metres (50 feet).
The main prey of snow leopards are wild mountain sheep and goats such as blue sheep and ibex.
Snow leopards have pale green or grey eyes, while most other big cats have yellow or amber eyes.
Snow leopards live in 12 countries, including China, Mongolia, India, Nepal, Bhutan and Pakistan.
In 2017 the IUCN, the global conservation body, moved the snow leopard from Endangered to Vulnerable.
Relative to its skull size, the clouded leopard has the longest canine teeth of any living cat.
Clouded leopards can climb down tree trunks head-first, something very few cats can do.
A clouded leopard can hang upside down from a branch by its hind feet, thanks to flexible ankle joints.
The clouded leopard is named for the large cloud-shaped blotches on its coat.
Despite its name, the clouded leopard is not a type of leopard. It belongs to its own genus, Neofelis.
The Sunda clouded leopard of Borneo and Sumatra was recognised as a separate species in 2006.
Clouded leopards have short legs, big paws and a very long tail, all suited to life in the trees.
Clouded leopards are found from the Himalayan foothills through Southeast Asia to southern China.
Clouded leopards are so secretive that much of what is known about them comes from animals in zoos.
Clouded leopards are listed as Vulnerable, threatened by forest loss and hunting for their skins.
The sand cat is the only cat that lives mainly in true deserts, from the Sahara to Central Asia.
Thick fur on the soles of a sand cat's feet protects it from scorching sand and hides its footprints.
Sand cats can go for weeks without drinking, getting the water they need from their prey.
Sand cats hunt at night and spend the hot desert day in burrows, often ones dug by other animals.
Sand cats are skilled snake hunters and will even take on venomous vipers.
A sand cat has large, low-set ears that pick up the faint sounds of prey moving under the sand.
A sand cat's pale sandy coat blends in so well that the cat almost vanishes when it crouches on the sand.
Sand cats are about the size of a small house cat but have a broader head and shorter legs.
Sand cats cope with desert extremes, from freezing nights to ground temperatures above 50 C (122 F).
The sand cat's scientific name, Felis margarita, honours a French officer whose expedition found it in 1858.
Pallas's cat, also called the manul, has round pupils, unlike the vertical slit pupils of most small cats.
Pallas's cat has the longest and densest fur of any wild cat, which keeps it warm on the cold Asian steppes.
The manul's thick fur makes it look much bigger than it is. Underneath, it weighs about as much as a house cat.
Pallas's cat is named after Peter Simon Pallas, a German naturalist who described it in 1776.
Pallas's cats have flat faces and low-set ears, which help them peer over rocks without being seen.
The manul's flat face and round pupils give it an unusually expressive, often grumpy-looking appearance.
Pallas's cats live across the steppes and mountains of Central Asia, from Iran to Mongolia and western China.
Pallas's cats mainly hunt pikas, small rabbit relatives, and often ambush them at their burrow entrances.
Pallas's cats are poor runners and rely on hiding and ambush rather than speed to catch prey.
Droppings collected on Mount Everest showed that Pallas's cats live there at over 5,000 metres (16,400 feet).
Fishing cats live in the wetlands of South and Southeast Asia and are strong, willing swimmers.
A fishing cat will dive head-first into water to catch fish and can swim underwater.
Fishing cats have been seen tapping the water's surface with a paw, apparently to lure fish within reach.
A fishing cat has a double-layered coat. A dense inner layer keeps its skin dry while it swims.
The fishing cat is about twice the size of a house cat, with a stocky body and a short tail.
A fishing cat's claws do not retract fully, so their tips show even when the cat is relaxed.
The fishing cat is the state animal of West Bengal in India.
Fishing cats are listed as Vulnerable, mainly because their wetland homes are being drained and built over.
Fishing cats eat more than fish. They also take frogs, crabs, snakes, birds and small mammals.
Fishing cats live in the mangrove forests of the Sundarbans, shared by India and Bangladesh.
Fishing cats also live in Sri Lanka, where they have been recorded in the suburbs of Colombo.
The black-footed cat of southern Africa is the smallest wild cat in Africa, weighing just 1 to 2.5 kg.
The black-footed cat is often called the deadliest cat on Earth: about 60 percent of its hunts end in a kill.
A black-footed cat may catch 10 to 14 small animals in a single night, far more than most cats.
Black-footed cats spend the day in abandoned termite mounds or burrows, earning the nickname anthill tiger.
Only the soles of a black-footed cat's feet are black. The rest of its coat is tawny with dark spots.
Black-footed cats are found only in the dry grasslands and scrub of South Africa, Namibia and Botswana.
A black-footed cat may walk 8 km (5 miles) or more in a single night of hunting.
Despite its tiny size, a black-footed cat has been known to kill Cape hares heavier than itself.
Black-footed cats are listed as Vulnerable, with fewer than 10,000 adults thought to remain in the wild.
Black-footed cats are strictly nocturnal and almost never seen by day, which makes them hard to study.
The rusty-spotted cat of India and Sri Lanka is the smallest wild cat, weighing about 1 to 1.6 kg as an adult.
A full-grown rusty-spotted cat is only about half the size of a typical house cat.
The rusty-spotted cat is named for the rust-coloured spots that run along its grey-brown back and sides.
Rusty-spotted cats were long thought to live only in India and Sri Lanka, but they have also been found in Nepal.
Rusty-spotted cats are nimble climbers and hunt mainly at night for rodents, birds, lizards and insects.
Rusty-spotted cats are sometimes called the hummingbird of the cat family because they are so small and quick.
The flat-headed cat of Southeast Asia has a long, flattened skull and small ears set low on its head.
Flat-headed cats live along rivers and swamps in Borneo, Sumatra and the Malay Peninsula, feeding mainly on fish.
A flat-headed cat's eyes face forward and sit close together, giving it sharp depth perception for catching fish.
The flat-headed cat's teeth are unusually sharp and pointed for a cat, well suited to gripping slippery prey.
The flat-headed cat is listed as Endangered, mainly because the lowland wetlands it depends on are being cleared.
The flat-headed cat is one of the least known cats in the world, and most recent records come from camera traps.
The margay can rotate its ankles up to 180 degrees, letting it run head-first down a tree trunk like a squirrel.
A margay can hang from a branch by one hind foot, leaving its front paws free to grab prey.
Margays spend most of their lives in the trees of Central and South American rainforests.
A margay looks like a small ocelot but has a longer tail and a lighter build suited to climbing.
In Brazil, a margay was seen mimicking the calls of baby pied tamarin monkeys, apparently to lure the adults.
Margays have very large eyes for their size, which help them hunt birds, tree frogs and small mammals at night.
Margays usually give birth to a single kitten, one reason their numbers recover slowly from losses.
With its long body, short legs and small flat head, the jaguarundi looks more like an otter than a typical cat.
Jaguarundis come in two main colours, dark grey and reddish brown, and both can appear in the same litter.
Unlike most small cats, the jaguarundi is active mainly by day and often hunts in the morning.
The jaguarundi's closest living relative is the puma, even though it is not much bigger than a house cat.
Jaguarundis are unusually vocal for a cat, with at least 13 distinct calls recorded.
The jaguarundi's coat is plain, with no spots or stripes, which is rare among the small cats of the Americas.
The kodkod, or guina, of Chile and Argentina is the smallest cat in the Americas, weighing about 2 kg.
Kodkods live mainly in the temperate rainforests of southern Chile and are expert climbers.
Black kodkods are fairly common, and their spots can still be seen faintly in the right light.
Kodkods feed mainly on rodents, birds and lizards, and sometimes raid farm chicken coops.
Kodkods are listed as Vulnerable, mainly because the Chilean forests they depend on are being logged.
The oncilla, also called the little spotted cat, is about the size of a house cat but more slender.
Oncillas live in forests from Costa Rica to northern Argentina, often in cool cloud forests high in the mountains.
In 2013 genetic studies showed that Brazil's oncillas are really two separate species that do not interbreed.
Oncillas are hard to tell from margays and ocelots, but they are smaller and have a narrower face.
Geoffroy's cats have been seen standing on their hind legs to scan their surroundings, using the tail for support.
Geoffroy's cats range from Bolivia and southern Brazil down to Patagonia, further south than almost any other cat.
Black Geoffroy's cats are common in some areas, and the species was once heavily hunted for its spotted fur.
The Andean mountain cat lives high in the Andes, usually above 3,000 metres, among rocks and sparse grass.
The Andean mountain cat is one of the rarest cats in the world, with fewer than 1,500 adults thought to survive.
The Andean mountain cat has a long, bushy tail with dark rings that helps it balance on rocky slopes.
The Andean mountain cat's main prey is the mountain viscacha, a rabbit-like rodent of the high Andes.
The pampas cat's coat varies so much across South America that some scientists split it into several species.
Despite its name, the pampas cat lives not only on grasslands but also in dry forests and high in the Andes.
Pampas cats have a ridge of longer fur running along the spine that can look like a short mane.
The bay cat is found only on the island of Borneo and is one of the least known cats on Earth.
The bay cat is so rare that scientists did not examine a live one until 1992, over a century after it was named.
Bay cats come in two colours, a rich chestnut red and a rarer grey, and have a long tail with a pale underside.
The bay cat is listed as Endangered as Borneo's forests are cleared for logging and oil palm plantations.
The Asian golden cat comes in many coat colours, from reddish gold to grey, black and even a spotted form.
The Asian golden cat's closest relative is the bay cat of Borneo, which looks like a smaller version of it.
Asian golden cats range from Nepal and northeast India through Southeast Asia to southern China and Sumatra.
The African golden cat lives only in the rainforests of West and Central Africa and is rarely seen.
Despite its name, the African golden cat may be reddish-brown, grey or occasionally black.
The African golden cat is closely related to the caracal and serval, not to the Asian golden cat.
The marbled cat of Southeast Asia looks like a miniature clouded leopard, with blotched fur and a very long tail.
A marbled cat's thick, bushy tail is about as long as its head and body combined.
Marbled cats are among the most tree-loving of all cats and hunt squirrels and birds high in the canopy.
The marbled cat is about the size of a house cat, and its closest relatives are the bay cat and Asian golden cat.
The leopard cat is the most widespread wild cat in Asia, found from Pakistan to Siberia and Indonesia.
The leopard cat is the wild parent species of the Bengal breed, but in the wild it is a shy forest hunter.
Leopard cats are about the size of a house cat, with longer legs and two white stripes running up from the eyes.
Leopard cats in the cold north, such as the Amur region, are bigger and paler than those in the tropics.
The Iriomote cat lives only on Iriomote, a small Japanese island, and is now treated as a type of leopard cat.
The Iriomote cat was unknown to science until 1965, and only about 100 of them are thought to exist.
Iriomote cats live on an island of under 300 square km, one of the smallest ranges of any wild cat.
Despite its name, the jungle cat prefers reed beds, marshes and riverbanks to dense forest.
The jungle cat, also called the reed cat, ranges from Egypt through the Middle East to Southeast Asia.
The jungle cat is the largest living member of Felis, the group of small cats that also includes the wildcat.
The Chinese mountain cat is found only in China, on the eastern edge of the Tibetan Plateau.
The Chinese mountain cat was first photographed in the wild in 2007, by camera traps in Sichuan.
The Chinese mountain cat has a sandy coat, faint stripes, a bushy ringed tail and small tufts on its ear tips.

European wildcats live in forests from Portugal and Scotland east to the Caucasus, and also on Sicily.
The Scottish wildcat is Britain's only native wild cat, and interbreeding with domestic cats is its biggest threat.

The African wildcat is the wild ancestor of the domestic cat, and the two can still interbreed freely.
African wildcats have longer legs than house cats, giving them a taller, more upright look when sitting.
The African wildcat has reddish fur on the backs of its ears, a quick way to tell it from a tabby.

Thanks to captive breeding and releases, the Iberian lynx population passed 2,000 animals by 2024.
In 2024 the Iberian lynx was moved from Endangered to Vulnerable, a rare conservation success for a cat.
The Iberian lynx depends heavily on rabbits, which make up the great majority of its diet.
Diseases that wiped out wild rabbits in Spain were a major cause of the Iberian lynx's decline.
Iberian lynx are smaller than Eurasian lynx, with a more spotted coat and a beard of long fur on the cheeks.

The Eurasian lynx is the largest of the four lynx species and can weigh more than 25 kg (55 pounds).
Unlike the smaller lynxes, the Eurasian lynx often hunts deer, taking roe deer and even young red deer.

Bobcats thrive in a huge range of habitats, from swamps and deserts to forests and the edges of suburbs.
