The Abyssinian is named after Abyssinia, the old name for Ethiopia, but the breed was developed in Britain.
Abyssinians have a ticked coat, with several bands of colour on each hair, giving a warm shimmering look.
A cat named Zula, brought to England from Abyssinia in the 1860s, is often cited as the Abyssinian's founder.
The American Bobtail is a naturally short-tailed breed that was developed in the United States from the 1960s.
An American Bobtail's tail is short but clearly visible, and the standard says it should not reach below the hock.
The American Bobtail was bred to look like a bobcat, but it is a fully domestic cat with no wild blood.
The American Curl's ears curl backwards in a smooth arc, a natural mutation first seen in California in 1981.
The first American Curl was a stray longhaired black kitten named Shulamith, found in Lakewood, California.
American Curl kittens are born with straight ears that begin to curl within days and set by about four months.
An American Curl's ears should curl in an arc of at least 90 degrees but no more than 180 degrees.
The American Shorthair descends from cats brought to North America by early European settlers to control rats.
Until 1966 the American Shorthair was called the Domestic Shorthair, a name changed to set it apart from moggies.
The American Shorthair was among the first five breeds recognised by the CFA when the association began in 1906.
The silver classic tabby is the best known American Shorthair, though the breed has over 80 colours and patterns.
The American Wirehair began with one kitten born on a farm in upstate New York in 1966 with a springy, wiry coat.
An American Wirehair's hairs are crimped, hooked or bent, down to the whiskers, so the coat feels like steel wool.
The American Wirehair is one of the rarest breeds, and its coat mutation has only been recorded in the United States.
The Australian Mist was developed in Australia from the 1970s by crossing Burmese, Abyssinian and domestic cats.
The Australian Mist was first called the Spotted Mist, and was renamed when marbled cats joined the spotted ones.
The Australian Mist was the first pedigree cat breed developed in Australia.
The Balinese is a longhaired Siamese, named for the graceful dancers of Bali, not for any link to the island.
The Balinese grew from longhaired kittens born in Siamese litters, and was established in the US in the 1950s.
Balinese cats have the pointed colouring and blue eyes of the Siamese, with a silky single coat and a plumed tail.
The Bengal was bred in the United States by crossing domestic cats with the Asian leopard cat, a small wild cat.
The Bengal's name comes from Prionailurus bengalensis, the scientific name of the Asian leopard cat.
Bengals must be several generations removed from their wild ancestor before registries allow them to be shown.
Bengals come in spotted and marbled patterns, and the rosette, a spot with a darker outline, is a prized marking.
The Birman is also called the Sacred Cat of Burma, after a legend about temple cats whose paws stayed pure white.
Birmans have a pointed coat, deep blue eyes and four pure white paws that breeders call gloves.
The white on a Birman's back paws runs up the back of the leg in a point, a marking that breeders call laces.
The Birman was first recognised as a breed in France in 1925.
The Bombay was created in Kentucky in the 1950s by crossing Burmese with black American Shorthairs.
The Bombay is named after the Indian city of Bombay, now Mumbai, as a nod to the black leopards of India.
A Bombay has a jet black coat right to the roots, copper or gold eyes and a sheen often compared to patent leather.
The British Longhair is the longhaired version of the British Shorthair, and the two can be born in the same litter.
The British Longhair's coat comes from Persians crossed into the British Shorthair to rebuild it after the world wars.
The British Shorthair is one of the oldest British breeds, developed from street and farm cats and shown in the 1870s.
The British Blue, with its dense grey-blue coat and copper eyes, is the most famous colour of the British Shorthair.
The British Shorthair has been the most popular pedigree breed registered with the GCCF in Britain for many years.
British Shorthairs mature slowly and may not reach their full size until they are three to five years old.
The Burmese breed in the West descends from a brown cat named Wong Mau, brought from Burma to San Francisco in 1930.
Wong Mau, the founding Burmese, turned out to be a Burmese-Siamese hybrid of the kind now called Tonkinese.
The classic Burmese colour is sable, a rich dark brown, which is called brown in Britain.
Burmese cats have a heavy, muscular body under a short satin coat, often described as a brick wrapped in silk.
The Burmilla came from an accidental mating between a Chinchilla Persian and a lilac Burmese in Britain in 1981.
The Burmilla's name combines Burmese and Chinchilla, the two breeds behind it.
Burmillas have a shaded or tipped silver coat and dark lines around the eyes that look like eyeliner.
The Chartreux is an old French breed with a blue-grey woolly coat and copper to orange eyes.
The Chartreux is known for its smiling expression, which comes from its rounded head and tapered muzzle.
Legend links the Chartreux to the Carthusian monks of France, but there is no proof the monks ever bred the cats.
The Chartreux is traditionally a quiet breed, and many of these cats chirp rather than meow.
The Chausie's name comes from Felis chaus, the scientific name of the jungle cat, one of its ancestors.
The Chausie is a tall, long-legged breed developed from crosses between domestic cats and the wild jungle cat.
Chausies are accepted in only three colours: solid black, black grizzled ticked tabby and brown ticked tabby.
The Cornish Rex began in 1950 with a curly-coated kitten named Kallibunker, born on a farm in Cornwall, England.
The Cornish Rex has only soft undercoat hairs, with no guard hairs, and they lie in tight waves like a washboard.
The word Rex in curly-coated cat breed names was borrowed from rex rabbits, which have a similar plush coat.
Cornish Rex cats have an arched back, tucked-up waist and long legs, giving them the outline of a small greyhound.
The Cymric is the longhaired Manx, and its name is the Welsh word for Welsh, though the breed is from the Isle of Man.
Some registries, including the CFA, treat the Cymric as a longhaired division of the Manx, not a separate breed.
The Devon Rex began in 1960 with a curly kitten named Kirlee, born near a disused tin mine in Devon, England.
Mating the first Devon Rex to Cornish Rex cats gave only straight-coated kittens, proving the two curl genes differ.
The Devon Rex is often called a pixie cat because of its large low-set ears, wide eyes and short muzzle.
Devon Rex whiskers are often short, curly and brittle, and some cats appear to have almost no whiskers at all.
The Donskoy is a hairless breed from Russia, first found in 1987 in Rostov-on-Don, the city it is named after.
Unlike the Sphynx, the Donskoy owes its hairlessness to a dominant gene, so one hairless parent can pass it on.
The Donskoy breed started with a kitten named Varvara, rescued from boys in Rostov-on-Don, who lost her fur as she grew.
The Donskoy is also known as the Don Sphynx, though it is not related to the Canadian Sphynx.
The Egyptian Mau is one of the few naturally spotted domestic breeds, with spots on the coat and on the skin beneath it.
Mau is the ancient Egyptian word for cat, which gives the Egyptian Mau its name.
The Egyptian Mau carries a mark on its forehead that fanciers call the scarab beetle mark, shaped like the letter M.
An exiled Russian princess, Nathalie Troubetskoy, brought the first Egyptian Maus from Italy to the US in 1956.
The European Shorthair is the pedigree form of the ordinary house cat of mainland Europe, with its own FIFe standard.
Until 1982 FIFe grouped the European Shorthair with the British Shorthair under a single standard.
The European Shorthair is less extreme than the British Shorthair, with a longer face and a less heavy body.
The Exotic Shorthair is a shorthaired Persian, nicknamed the lazy man's Persian because its coat needs less grooming.
The Exotic Shorthair was created in the US in the 1950s and 1960s by crossing Persians with American Shorthairs.
Exotic Shorthairs are judged to the same standard as the Persian in every respect except the coat.
The Exotic Shorthair was accepted for championship by the CFA in 1967.
The Havana Brown is a solid chocolate brown cat developed in England in the 1950s from Siamese and black domestic cats.
The Havana Brown may be named for the colour of Havana cigars or after the Havana rabbit, which has the same brown coat.
The Havana Brown is one of the few breeds whose standard requires the whiskers to be brown to match the coat.
The Havana Brown has a distinctive muzzle that the standard compares to a corn cob or a light bulb.
The Highlander is a recent breed with curled ears and a naturally short tail, developed in the US in the 2000s.
The Highlander was known as the Highland Lynx until it was renamed in 2005.
The Highlander has no wild cat blood despite its lynx-like look, which was bred in from domestic cats alone.
The Himalayan is a Persian with the Siamese pointed pattern and blue eyes, created by crossing the two breeds.
The Himalayan is named after the Himalayan rabbit, which has the same dark points on a pale body, not the mountains.
In the CFA the Himalayan is a colour division of the Persian, while some other registries list it as a breed of its own.
In Britain the Himalayan is called the Colourpoint Persian.
The Japanese Bobtail's short pom-pom tail comes from a recessive gene, and every tail is unique in its kinks and curves.
The Japanese Bobtail has lived in Japan for centuries and appears in old Japanese paintings and woodblock prints.
The mi-ke, or tricolour, of white with patches of black and red is the most famous Japanese Bobtail colour.
The Japanese Bobtail was brought to the United States in 1968 by Elizabeth Freret, and the CFA recognised it in 1976.
The Javanese is a longhaired Siamese-type cat in colours the Balinese does not allow, named after the island of Java.
In 2008 the CFA merged the Javanese into the Balinese breed as a colour division.
The Khao Manee is an ancient pure white breed from Thailand, and its name means white gem.
Khao Manee cats often have odd eyes, one blue and one gold or green, which breeders prize highly.
The Khao Manee is described in the Tamra Maew, an old Thai book of cat poems.
In Thailand the Khao Manee is also known as the Diamond Eye cat.
The Korat is a silver-blue cat from Thailand, named after Korat province, and is one of the oldest stable breeds.
In Thailand the Korat is called Si-Sawat and is traditionally given in pairs to brides as a token of good luck.
A Korat's blue coat is tipped with silver, which gives it a halo effect, and its eyes are a luminous green.
The Korat has a heart-shaped head when viewed from the front, a feature called for in the breed standard.
The Kurilian Bobtail is a natural breed from the Kuril Islands, a chain of islands between Russia and Japan.
The Kurilian Bobtail's pom-pom tail has between two and ten vertebrae, kinked and curved so that no two are the same.
The Kurilian Bobtail is popular in Russia and recognised by FIFe and TICA, but it is still rare in North America.
The LaPerm began in 1982 with a bald kitten named Curly, born on a farm in Oregon, who later grew a curly coat.
The LaPerm's curly coat is caused by a dominant gene, unlike the recessive curls of the Cornish and Devon Rex.
LaPerm kittens may be born bald or straight-coated and then grow curls, and some lose and regrow their coat.
The Lykoi, or werewolf cat, has a patchy coat with no hair on the face mask, from a natural mutation in domestic cats.
The Lykoi's name comes from the Greek word for wolves.
The Lykoi breed was founded in 2011 in Tennessee from kittens born to feral and shelter cats carrying the gene.
The Lykoi's coat is roan, a mix of coloured and white hairs that is common in horses but found in no other cat breed.
The Maine Coon is the official state cat of Maine, where it developed as a hardy farm cat.
The Maine Coon is one of the largest domestic breeds, with a shaggy coat, a bushy tail and tufted ears and toes.
Legend says the Maine Coon descends from a cat and a raccoon, which is impossible, but the name stuck.
A Maine Coon named Cosey won the top prize at the first big American cat show, held at Madison Square Garden in 1895.
The Manx comes from the Isle of Man, where its tailless gene spread through an isolated island cat population.
Manx cats are classed by tail: rumpy (no tail), rumpy riser (a small bump), stumpy (short) and longy (full tail).
The Manx tailless gene is dominant, and kittens with two copies do not survive, so Manx litters are often small.
Some Manx cats have a spinal condition called Manx syndrome, which is linked to the gene that shortens the tail.
The Munchkin is a short-legged breed caused by a natural dominant mutation that leaves the body a normal size.
The Munchkin breed was founded in Louisiana in 1983 from a short-legged stray named Blackberry.
The Munchkin is named after the small people of Oz in L. Frank Baum's book The Wonderful Wizard of Oz.
TICA accepted the Munchkin into its new breed programme in 1994, but the CFA, GCCF and FIFe do not recognise it.
The Nebelung is a longhaired blue cat with green eyes, bred to look like a longhaired Russian Blue.
The Nebelung's name comes from the German word Nebel, meaning mist or fog, for its soft blue-grey coat.
The Nebelung breed began in Denver in the 1980s with two longhaired blue kittens named Siegfried and Brunhilde.
The Norwegian Forest Cat has a water-shedding top coat over a woolly undercoat, built for Scandinavian winters.
In Norway the Norwegian Forest Cat is called the skogkatt, which simply means forest cat.
The Norwegian Forest Cat was accepted by FIFe in 1977 and reached the United States in 1979.
The Norwegian Forest Cat has a triangular head with a straight profile, which sets it apart from the Maine Coon.
The Ocicat is a spotted breed with no wild blood that appeared by accident in 1964 from Siamese and Abyssinian lines.
The Ocicat is named after the ocelot, a spotted wild cat, because of its look, although the two are unrelated.
The first Ocicat was named Tonga, and the breed was built from Siamese, Abyssinian and American Shorthair cats.
The breeder of the first Ocicat was trying to make an Abyssinian-pointed Siamese when a spotted kitten appeared.
The Oriental Shorthair is a Siamese-type cat in colours and patterns other than pointed, usually with green eyes.
Oriental Shorthairs come in more than 300 colour and pattern combinations.
The Oriental Shorthair was developed in Britain in the 1950s and 1960s from Siamese crossed with other breeds.
In Britain, solid-coloured Orientals were first given their own names, such as Havana for chocolate and Foreign White.
The Persian is one of the oldest and most popular pedigree breeds, and was shown at the first cat show in 1871.
The Persian's name refers to Persia, now Iran, from where longhaired cats were first brought to Europe in the 1600s.
The Persian is known for its long flowing coat, round face and short nose, and comes in a huge range of colours.
The Persian's flat face can make breathing and tear drainage harder, so many breeders now favour a more moderate head.
The Persian was the most registered breed with the CFA for many years.
The Peterbald was created in St Petersburg, Russia, in 1994 by crossing a Donskoy with an Oriental Shorthair.
The Peterbald has a long, slender Oriental build and may be bald, flocked, velour or brush-coated.
Peterbald kittens can be born with hair and lose it as they grow, because the hair-loss gene is dominant and variable.
The Pixie-bob is a bobtailed breed from the Pacific Northwest, bred to look like a bobcat, though it is fully domestic.
The Pixie-bob breed is named after a kitten called Pixie, born in 1986 to a bobtailed cat in Washington state.
The Pixie-bob is one of the few breeds whose standard allows polydactyl cats, with up to seven toes on a foot.
The Pixie-bob is shown only as a brown spotted tabby, often with a woolly coat and a heavy brow that gives a wild look.
The Ragamuffin split from the Ragdoll breed in 1994 and allows a wider range of colours and patterns than the Ragdoll.
The Ragamuffin gained championship status in the CFA in 2011.
Unlike the Ragdoll, the Ragamuffin may be any colour and pattern, with or without white, and need not have blue eyes.
The Ragdoll was developed in California in the 1960s by Ann Baker, and is named for the way many go limp when picked up.
Ragdolls are pointed cats with blue eyes, and they come in three patterns: colourpoint, mitted and bicolour.
All Ragdoll kittens are born white and develop their colour over the first weeks, with the full colour taking years.
The Ragdoll's founder was a white longhaired cat named Josephine, who lived in Riverside, California.
In recent years the Ragdoll has been the most registered breed with the CFA, ahead of the Maine Coon and Exotic.
The Russian Blue has a dense, silver-tipped blue coat and vivid green eyes.
The Russian Blue is thought to come from the port of Arkhangelsk in Russia, and was once called the Archangel Cat.
The Russian Blue was shown at the Crystal Palace in London in 1875 under the name Archangel Cat.
Russian Blues were crossed with Siamese during the Second World War when numbers fell, then bred back to the old type.
Russian Blues have mauve or lavender-pink paw pads, unlike the grey pads of most other blue cats.
The Savannah is a cross between domestic cats and the serval, a tall, long-legged wild cat of Africa.
The first Savannah, herself named Savannah, was born in 1986 to a Siamese mother and a serval father.
TICA accepted the Savannah for championship status in 2012.
Savannahs are labelled by generation: an F1 cat has a serval parent, an F2 a serval grandparent, and so on.
Owning a Savannah is restricted or banned in some places because of its wild ancestry, often depending on generation.
The Scottish Fold descends from a barn cat named Susie, found in Scotland in 1961 with ears that folded forward.
The Scottish Fold's ears come from a dominant gene that affects cartilage throughout the body, not only in the ears.
Scottish Fold kittens are born with straight ears, and the fold appears at about three weeks in those with the gene.
The GCCF stopped registering Scottish Folds in the early 1970s over concerns about cartilage and joint problems.
Scottish Folds with straight ears are called Scottish Straights, and breeders use them to avoid pairing two folds.
The Selkirk Rex has a loose, curly coat and a sturdy body, and is sometimes called the cat in sheep's clothing.
The Selkirk Rex began in 1987 with a curly kitten from a Montana animal shelter, later named Miss DePesto.
The Selkirk Rex is said to be the only cat breed named after a person: its founder named it after her stepfather.
Unlike the Cornish and Devon Rex, the Selkirk Rex curl gene is dominant, so one curly parent can give curly kittens.
The Siamese comes from Thailand, formerly Siam, where pointed cats were described in old manuscripts.
Siamese cats shown at the Crystal Palace in 1871 were described by one writer as an unnatural nightmare kind of cat.
The four classic Siamese colours are seal, chocolate, blue and lilac point.
Many early Siamese had crossed eyes and kinked tails, which are now considered faults in the show ring.
The Siamese is an ancestor of many breeds, including the Balinese, Oriental, Himalayan, Tonkinese and Snowshoe.
The Siberian is Russia's native forest cat, with a thick triple coat, and is often called the national cat of Russia.
The Siberian was first exported from Russia in 1990, after the opening up of the Soviet Union made it possible.
The Neva Masquerade is the colourpoint version of the Siberian, named for the Neva river in St Petersburg.
The Siberian is slow to mature and can take about five years to reach its full size.
The Singapura is one of the smallest breeds, with a sepia ticked coat and large eyes and ears for its size.
The Singapura takes its name from the Malay name for Singapore, where its ancestors lived as street cats.
The Singapura is shown in only one colour, sepia agouti, a warm ivory ticked with dark brown.
The Singapura was brought to the United States in the 1970s by Hal and Tommy Meadow, and the CFA recognised it in 1988.
The Snowshoe is a pointed cat with white feet, bred in Philadelphia in the 1960s from Siamese and American Shorthairs.
The Snowshoe's white markings are hard to breed to the standard, which is one reason the breed remains rare.
Snowshoes usually have an inverted V of white on the face and white boots on all four feet.
The Sokoke is a rare natural breed from the coast of Kenya, named after the Arabuko Sokoke forest.
The Sokoke has a blotched tabby coat with ticked hairs, giving a look often compared to wood grain or tree bark.
The Sokoke was first bred from cats found in Kenya in 1978 by Jeni Slater, and was taken to Denmark in the 1980s.
The Somali is the longhaired version of the Abyssinian, with a full brush tail that earns it the nickname fox cat.
The Somali was named after Somalia, Ethiopia's neighbour, to echo the Abyssinian's name.
The Somali was developed in the 1960s and 1970s from longhaired kittens that sometimes appeared in Abyssinian litters.
The Sphynx is a hairless breed that began in Toronto in 1966 with a hairless kitten named Prune.
The Sphynx is not truly bald: its skin is covered in fine down that feels like warm suede or a peach.
The Sphynx's hairlessness comes from a recessive gene, and another form of the same gene gives the Devon Rex its coat.
A Sphynx's skin shows the colour and pattern that the cat's fur would have had.
The Sphynx was granted championship status by the CFA in 2002.
The Thai is TICA's name for the old-style Siamese, with a rounder head and sturdier body than the modern show Siamese.
In Thailand the Thai cat is called the Wichienmaat, a name also used there for the Siamese.
The Thai is sometimes called the Old-Style, Classic or Applehead Siamese.
The Tonkinese is a cross between the Siamese and the Burmese, blending the colouring and build of both.
Tonkinese come in three patterns: pointed, mink and solid, and mink cats have aqua eyes.
The Tonkinese is named after the Tonkin region of Vietnam, although the breed has no connection to it.
When two mink Tonkinese are bred, on average half the kittens are mink, a quarter pointed and a quarter solid.
The Tonkinese was recognised in 1971 by the Canadian Cat Association, the first registry to accept it.
The Toyger is a striped breed designed to look like a tiny tiger, created in the United States from the 1980s.
The Toyger was developed from Bengals and striped domestic shorthairs, and has no tiger blood at all.
The Toyger's name is a blend of the words toy and tiger.
TICA accepted the Toyger for championship status in 2007.
The Turkish Angora is an ancient longhaired breed from Turkey, named after Ankara, which was once called Angora.
Ankara Zoo has run a breeding programme for many years to preserve the white Turkish Angora.
Turkish Angoras almost vanished as a pure breed in the early 1900s after being crossed with Persians.
White Turkish Angoras with blue or odd eyes are the best known, and the CFA accepted only white cats until 1978.
The Turkish Van comes from the Lake Van region of eastern Turkey and is famous for liking to swim.
The Turkish Van's white body with colour only on the head and tail is so distinctive it is called the van pattern.
Turkish Vans often have odd eyes, one blue and one amber.
The Turkish Van was brought to Britain in 1955 by two photographers who were given a pair of kittens in Turkey.
The Ukrainian Levkoy is a hairless breed with folded ears, created in Ukraine from Donskoy and Scottish Fold crosses.
The Ukrainian Levkoy is named after the levkoy plant, whose bent leaves are said to resemble the cat's folded ears.
The Ukrainian Levkoy is recognised by Ukrainian and Russian clubs but not yet by the major international registries.
The York Chocolate is a longhaired chocolate or lilac breed developed in New York State in the 1980s from farm cats.
The York Chocolate began with a kitten named Brownie, born in 1983 to a black longhaired farm cat.
The York Chocolate has been recognised by only a few registries, and the breed is now very rare.
The first proper cat show was held at the Crystal Palace in London in July 1871, organised by the artist Harrison Weir.
Harrison Weir, who organised the first cat show, wrote the first breed standards, which he called Points of Excellence.
Harrison Weir founded the National Cat Club in 1887, the first cat registry, and became its first president.
The first major American cat show was held at Madison Square Garden in New York in 1895.
The Cat Fanciers' Association, or CFA, was founded in the United States in 1906.
The Governing Council of the Cat Fancy (GCCF) was formed in Britain in 1910 and remains the main UK registry.
FIFe, the Federation Internationale Feline, was founded in 1949 and unites cat clubs from many countries.
The International Cat Association (TICA) was founded in 1979 in the United States and calls itself a genetic registry.
At a cat show each cat is judged against a written breed standard, not directly against the other cats present.
A breed standard describes the ideal cat of a breed and gives points for each feature, usually adding up to 100.
A pedigree is a cat's family tree listing its registered ancestors, while a breed standard describes the ideal cat.
Many cat shows have a household pet class, where non-pedigree cats are judged on condition and temperament.
Domestic shorthair and domestic longhair are the terms for non-pedigree cats, sorted only by coat length.
Moggy is the British word for a cat of no particular breed.
Pedigree cats are a minority: by most estimates only a few percent of pet cats are pedigreed, and the rest are moggies.
The CFA recognises about 45 breeds and TICA more than 70, and the numbers change as new breeds are accepted.
Show titles such as Champion and Grand Champion are earned by winning a set number of awards under different judges.
Cat fanciers call a breeding female a queen, a breeding male a stud, and a kitten's parents its dam and sire.
FIFe and the GCCF do not register the Scottish Fold, while the CFA and TICA do.
The Persian, Siamese and Manx were all among the breeds shown at the very first cat show in 1871.
In recent years the most registered breed has been the Ragdoll in the CFA and the British Shorthair in the GCCF.
The word cobby, used in breed standards, means a short, compact, heavy-boned body like the Persian's.
The classic Abyssinian colour is called ruddy in North America and usual in Britain.
American Bobtail tails vary in length and shape from cat to cat, often with kinks and bumps.
The American Curl comes in longhair and shorthair varieties and is accepted in every colour and pattern.
Apart from its coat, the American Wirehair is judged to much the same standard as the American Shorthair it came from.
The Australian Mist's spots or marbling seem to sit on a misty background because the coat hairs are ticked.
Some Bengals have a glitter coat, where translucent hair tips make the coat sparkle in the light.
The Birman nearly died out in Europe during the Second World War, and the breed was rebuilt from a handful of cats.
Many Birman breeders follow a French custom: every kitten born in one year gets a name starting with the same letter.
The British Shorthair's thick plush coat is described as crisp in the standard and stands away from the body.
British Shorthairs have a round face with full cheeks, a stocky body and a calm, easygoing nature.
British and American Burmese differ in type: the American cat is rounder, the British more moderate and slender.
The Burmilla was first recognised in Britain, and it gained championship status in the CFA in 2014.
The Chartreux has a dense double coat with a woolly texture that breaks open like a sheep's fleece when the cat moves.
The Cornish Rex coat gene is recessive, so two Cornish Rex parents produce only curly-coated kittens.
Donskoy cats come in several coat types, from fully hairless rubber bald to short velour or wiry brush coats.
The Egyptian Mau is shown in the CFA in three colours: silver, bronze and smoke.
The Egyptian Mau has a flap of loose skin from the flank to the back knee, which lets it lengthen its stride.
Egyptian Maus have light green eyes, described as gooseberry green, which may take over a year to develop fully.
Because the longhair gene is recessive, some Exotic Shorthair litters include longhaired kittens.
In Britain the Havana became part of the Oriental Shorthair, while American cats kept the Havana Brown name.
The Himalayan was developed in the 1930s to 1950s by breeders in the United States and Britain.
A Japanese Bobtail's hind legs are longer than its front legs, but they bend so the back stays level when it stands.
The first Korats in the West arrived in the United States in 1959 as a gift to an American breeder.
The LaPerm's name refers to the hair perm, because its curls look like a permanent wave.
TICA granted the Lykoi championship status in 2017.
Lykoi cats moult heavily at times and can become almost bald, then grow the coat back again.
The Maine Coon is sometimes called the gentle giant for its size and friendly nature.
Many Maine Coons have polydactyl ancestors, and some registries now accept extra-toed Maine Coons.
The Manx standard calls for roundness everywhere: a round head, round eyes, round cheeks and a rounded rump.
Short-legged cats were reported in Britain in the 1940s and in Russia in the 1950s, before the Munchkin breed existed.
The Norwegian Forest Cat nearly vanished as a distinct type in the Second World War and was rebuilt in the 1970s.
The Ocicat is accepted in twelve colours: tawny, chocolate, cinnamon, blue, lavender and fawn, each also in silver.
The Oriental Shorthair was accepted for championship by the CFA in 1977.
Traditional or doll-face Persians have the older, less flattened look, and some breeders specialise in them.
The Chinchilla is a silver-tipped Persian with green eyes, treated as a separate breed by the GCCF in Britain.
Peterbalds have long oval toes with webbing between them, which lets them pick up and hold small objects.
Ragamuffins are large, plush cats with a sweet expression and are known for a docile, relaxed temperament.
Ann Baker trademarked the Ragdoll name and set up her own registry, which split the breed's early breeders.
The Russian Blue's double coat stands out from the body, and a pattern traced in it with a finger stays until smoothed.
The Russian Blue is often said to have a slight upturn at the corners of the mouth that gives it a Mona Lisa smile.
The condition linked to the Scottish Fold gene is called osteochondrodysplasia, which affects cartilage and bone growth.
In Scotland the breed was first called the lop-eared cat, after lop-eared rabbits, before it became the Scottish Fold.
The Selkirk Rex comes in longhair and shorthair varieties, and both have curly whiskers.
Selkirk Rex kittens are born curly, and the coat straightens for a time before the curls return at 8 to 10 months.
Modern show Siamese have a wedge-shaped head, large ears and a long tubular body, far slimmer than cats of the 1950s.
Singapore has used the Singapura as a tourism mascot under the name Kucinta, meaning love cat.

Cat shows have classes for kittens, adults and neuters, so that neutered pets can compete for titles of their own.
A cat registry records births and pedigrees and sets the rules for showing, while affiliated clubs run the shows.

The term cat fancy means the hobby of breeding and showing pedigree cats, and its followers are called cat fanciers.
Natural breeds are those that developed on their own in one region, such as the Maine Coon, Siberian and Korat.
Hybrid breeds such as the Bengal and Savannah descend partly from small wild cats, and some registries refuse them.
Judges handle every cat, feeling the bone structure and coat, because much of a standard cannot be judged by eye alone.
A cattery name, registered with a registry, is the prefix used in the names of all the kittens a breeder produces.
The Siamese, Persian and Maine Coon are among the oldest recognised breeds, and the Lykoi and Toyger among the newest.
At TICA shows a cat competes within its breed and then, in allbreed rings, against every other breed for best cat.
