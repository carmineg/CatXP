The English word cat comes from the Late Latin cattus, which replaced the older Latin word for a cat, feles.
The word feline comes from feles, the Latin word the Romans used for cats before cattus took over.
Irish cat, Welsh cath, German Katze, Polish kot and Basque katu all share the Latin root cattus.
In French a cat is a chat, in Spanish and Portuguese a gato, in Italian a gatto and in Dutch a kat.
In Russian a male cat is a kot and a female cat is a koshka.
The Japanese word for cat is neko, and Japanese cats are said to go nyan rather than meow.
In Mandarin Chinese the word for cat is mao, which sounds much like the noise a cat makes.
The Arabic word for a cat is qitt for a male and qitta for a female.
In Hindi and Urdu a cat is a billi, in Finnish a kissa, in Turkish a kedi and in Tagalog a pusa.
Kitten came into English in the Middle Ages from an Old French word that was a diminutive of chat.
Puss, a word for calling a cat, has been used in English since the 1500s and gave Puss in Boots his name.
Moggy is a British word for an ordinary house cat of no particular breed.
Tabby comes from Attabiyah, a district of Baghdad known for striped silk. The cat was named after the cloth.
Catgut, used for violin strings and surgical stitches, was made from the guts of sheep and goats, never cats.
The cat o' nine tails was a whip of nine knotted cords used for punishment in the British navy and army.
A catwalk was a narrow walkway on a ship, bridge or stage long before the word was used for fashion runways.
A catnap is a short, light sleep, named for the way cats doze during the day.
The word copycat, for someone who imitates others, is recorded in American English from the late 1800s.
A cat burglar is a thief who climbs in through upper windows or roofs, a term that dates from the early 1900s.
The cat's pajamas, the cat's whiskers and the cat's meow were all 1920s American slang for something excellent.
In jazz slang of the 1930s a cat was a musician or a fan, and a cool cat was someone admirably relaxed.
Cab Calloway's Hepster's Dictionary of 1938 defined a cat as a swing musician or a lover of swing music.
The saying raining cats and dogs was in print by the 1700s, and nobody knows for certain where it came from.
The question about a cat having got someone's tongue is recorded from the mid-1800s, and its origin is unknown.
To let the cat out of the bag, meaning to give away a secret, appears in print from the 1760s.
Curiosity killed the cat dates from the late 1800s. The older form, from the 1590s, was care killed the cat.
In the old saying care killed the cat, used by Shakespeare and Ben Jonson, care meant worry.
A cat may look at a king, meaning even the lowly have some rights, appears in an English proverb collection of 1546.
The proverb when the cat's away the mice will play has been used in English since at least the early 1600s.
No room to swing a cat has described a cramped space since the 1660s. Its link to the cat o' nine tails is unproven.
Like herding cats, for a hopeless attempt to organize people, was popularized by a US television advert in 2000.
The phrase grin like a Cheshire cat was recorded in the 1780s, long before Lewis Carroll's Alice met the cat.
A fat cat, meaning a wealthy backer or a well-paid boss, is American slang recorded from the 1920s.
In the catbird seat, meaning in a strong position, was popularized by the baseball broadcaster Red Barber.
Catty-corner comes from cater-corner, with cater from the French word for four. It has nothing to do with cats.
Caterpillar probably comes from an Old French word meaning hairy cat, chatepelose.
Catkins, the fluffy flower spikes of willows and hazels, take their name from a Dutch word meaning little cat.
Catfish are named for the whisker-like feelers around their mouths, which look like a cat's whiskers.
The catcall was originally a squeaky whistle used by 1600s theatre audiences to jeer at bad plays.
A group of cats is sometimes called a clowder, a word that is a variant of clutter.
An old word for a litter of kittens is a kindle, from a verb meaning to give birth.
The word tomcat caught on after a 1760 book, The Life and Adventures of a Cat, made its hero Tom famous.
The French saying appeler un chat un chat, to call a cat a cat, means to speak plainly, like calling a spade a spade.
In German a Katzenjammer, literally the wailing of cats, is a hangover. The word is also used in English.
The Katzenjammer Kids, a comic strip begun in 1897 and one of the oldest in the world, contains no cats at all.
The Japanese saying neko no te mo karitai, wanting to borrow even a cat's paws, means being desperate for help.
Cat's cradle, the string game played between two people's hands, has a name whose origin nobody has explained.
Online, paying the cat tax means posting a photo of a cat after mentioning it in a post.
